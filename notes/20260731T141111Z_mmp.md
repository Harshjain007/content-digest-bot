# Deep-Dive Brief: MMP (Multi-Member Proportional Representation)

---

## 1. **TL;DR**
- MMP is a hybrid electoral system that combines single-member districts with proportional representation.
- It aims to balance local representation with fair party representation.
- It is used in several countries and is known for producing more diverse and representative legislatures.

---

## 2. **What it is**

**MMP** stands for **Mixed-Member Proportional Representation**. It is an electoral system used to determine the composition of a legislative body, such as a parliament or congress. The system blends two elements:

- **Single-Member Districts (SMDs):** Voters elect a representative for their local constituency.
- **Proportional Representation (PR):** Parties receive seats in proportion to the total votes they receive across the country.

The goal of MMP is to ensure that the composition of the legislature reflects the overall preferences of the electorate, while still allowing for local representation through single-member districts. It is designed to produce a more representative and inclusive political system compared to winner-takes-all models like first-past-the-post (FPTP).

---

## 3. **How it Works**

MMP operates in the following steps:

1. **Voters Cast Two Votes:**
   - **Local Vote:** For a single representative in their local district.
   - **Party Vote:** For a political party, which determines the party's share of the overall legislative seats.

2. **Election of Single-Member Districts:**
   - Each district elects one representative using a first-past-the-post (FPTP) system.
   - The candidate with the most votes in each district wins the seat.

3. **Allocation of Proportional Seats:**
   - The total number of seats allocated to each party is based on the percentage of the **party vote** they received.
   - A minimum threshold (often 5%) is usually required for a party to qualify for proportional representation seats.
   - The number of proportional seats is determined by subtracting the number of single-member district seats won by each party from the total number of seats in the legislature.

4. **Compensation for Disproportionality:**
   - If a party wins more single-member seats than their proportional share, they may lose some of those seats to ensure the overall distribution reflects the party vote.
   - This is known as the **compensation mechanism**, which helps to balance out the influence of local votes and party votes.

5. **Final Legislative Composition:**
   - The final legislative body is a combination of the single-member district representatives and the proportional representation seats.

### **Realistic Applications:**

## 5. **Key Details & Nuances**

- **Proportionality vs. Local Representation:** MMP seeks to balance both, but this can lead to complex trade-offs. For example, a party may win many local seats but still be underrepresented in the overall legislature.
- **Thresholds and Barriers:** The 5% threshold for proportional seats can marginalize smaller parties, even if they win many local seats.
- **Compensation Mechanism:** While it helps balance representation, it can be politically contentious and may be seen as unfair to some voters.
- **Complexity:** MMP is more complex to administer than FPTP, requiring careful calculation and often leading to disputes over seat allocation.
- **Coalition Governments:** MMP often leads to coalition governments, which can be more stable but also more fragile.

## 6. **Notable Examples / Tools / Players**

### **Countries Using MMP:**
- **New Zealand** (since 1996)
- **Germany** (for the Bundestag)
- **Scotland** (for the Scottish Parliament)
- **Canada** (for the Senate, though not for the House of Commons)

### **Key Players:**
- **New Zealand Labour Party:** One of the major parties in New Zealand, which has used MMP to build a broad coalition.
- **German Greens:** A party that has grown significantly under the proportional system.
- **Scottish National Party (SNP):** Gained significant influence through MMP in Scotland.

### **Alternatives to MMP:**
- **Pure Proportional Representation (PR):** Allocates seats strictly by party vote, without single-member districts.
- **First-Past-the-Post (FPTP):** Uses only local districts, leading to less proportional representation.
- **Mixed-Member Majoritarian (MMM):** Similar to MMP but with a different allocation mechanism (e.g., largest remainder method).
